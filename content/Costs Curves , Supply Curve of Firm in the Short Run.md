


Cost curves are the value function of the cost minimisation problem with respect to a given output. That is, it gives the minimum cost  to produce a given output. And if we consider the wages and rental rates to be constant, then we can write , C(y) as the cost function with dependence on output, y. 

now, total costs consists of two components : fixed costs and variable costs.
i.e.

$$
TC = FC + VC
$$

we are always interested in per unit analysis and marginal analysis in microeconomics as those affect the decisions in the larger scheme of things. That is we are interested in average costs (variable and fixed) and marginal costs here.

$$
ATC = AFC + AVC \quad \text{    and   } \quad MC =  \frac{d TC}{dy}
$$


Note that :  $$MC = MVC$$ 


By short run we mean some factor of production is constant. (eg. take the capital constant.) 

Firms don't respond to markets directly. Firms have the data of their own sales and at most they are aware about the market environment amidst the other firms. A Firm responds to the "demand faced by the firm" and that demand is altered by the market changes.

In the determination of firm's decisions whether to stay in business or not, this demand faced by the firm plays a key role.

But firstly, let's be clear about the shapes of the cost curves.
ATC curve will be determined by the shapes of AVC and AFC. 
AFC curve's shape is a decreasing curve, to be precise a rectangular hyperbola, as 
$$AFC. y = constant$$ .
Shape of AVC can be swiftly determined using calculus, but that will require some real work in here. But as usual we have an economic way of interpretation to find the explanations/results in economics, here we will use law of variable proportions :
###### Law of variable proportions: 
It states that in short run, the output of firm operates in three stages. First output increases with increase in input, with  increasing additional output that we get from additional unit of input i.e. with increasing marginal productivity. Then in second stage the output still increases with increase in input but the additional output that we get from additional unit of input is decreasing, i.e. marginal productivity decreasing, until it reaches its maximum output. In the third stage, the output starts to decrease, and the marginal productivity becomes negative i.e. the additional output is negative, when we put another input unit , which for example means output is getting reduced  by employing more labour.

Now , in general setting with only labour as factor of production , 

$$
VC = w.L \quad \implies AVC = \frac{w}{APL}
$$


where APL = average product of labour, denoted by AP in the graph.
so we will get a U shaped variable average cost curve. 
In the same spirit it can be proved, 

$$
VC = w.L \quad \implies MC = MVC =  \frac{w}{MPL}
$$


where MPL = marginal product of labour, denoted by MP in the graph.
so the MC curve will also be a U shaped curve with minimum below the AVC curve.

Sum of the U shaped AVC curve and a rectangular hyperbolic AFC curve will make the ATC curve a U shaped curve.

##### Relationship between MC curve and ATC curve :

Although we have already found the U shape of ATC it is worth while to consider its minimum point, so finding the derivative of ATC=TC/q wrt q we get : 

$$
\frac{d(\frac{TC}{q})}{q} = \frac{q.TC'-TC}{q^{2}}=\frac{MC-ATC}{q}
$$

equating it to zero gives us the condition that at min of ATC, MC= ATC. In other words, MC cuts ATC at the minimum point.

Before getting into firms decisions we need to address the following concepts :
##### Break even point , average revenue curve  :

Point of zero profit (where profit is total revenue - total cost ) , is also known as break-even point.

$$
\Pi = TR - TC = 0 \implies TR = TC \implies AR=ATC 
$$

i.e. average revenue = average total cost
But $$AR = \frac{p.q}{q} = p$$ which means the inverse demand function is the average revenue curve .Which means the point of intersection of demand curve face by the firm and ATC curve is the break even point. 

##### Profit maximising Output : 
$$
\frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial q } = \frac{\partial (TR - TC)}{\partial q } = 0 \implies MR = MC  
$$

so additional revenue that firm gets on production of one more unit must be equal to the additional cost of producing one more unit. Makes sense.
 

Now we can take up the ***case of a perfectly competitive market*** and the firm's decisions.


In  competitive market, the firm will be a price taker, that is, price will be independent of the output, so basically the demand faced by the firm would be a horizontal line as denoted here. Moreover, MR = price, which means that the point where the demand curve will intersect MC curve will be the point of profit maximisation output. But to actually have some positive profits, the demand curve must intersect above the min point of ATC. Because the firm will choose the output corresponding to the MC = P point, and  ATC=MC  at min of ATC curve, so min ATC = P = MC is the condition of zero profits i.e. break even point in Perfectly competitive market. Any point above that on the MC curve will give a positive profit inducing output. And graphically that profit  has been showcased as shaded region here.
Contrary to the general idea that below break even firm will shut down, actually a firm can still function in the short run, as long as it is above the variable cost curve. The reasoning stems from the fact that at q=0, total profit is -FC ; if the profit ($$py-VC(y) - FC$$)is less than i.e. loss is more than this then firm's operating even in short run is not possible i.e. for shutdown : 

$$
-FC > py-VC(y) - FC \implies AVC(y) = VC(y)> p
$$

so demand curve should be above min AVC to avoid shut down.


Note that the profit maximising quantity supplied by the firm is solely determined by the firm's marginal cost, as price is independent in perfect competition, here comes the first indication that supply curve has something to do with the MC curve. Everything can be proved via calculus but the general definition make everything clear, MC is the additional cost to produce one extra unit , or better put, the price at which producer is willing to sell the nth unit since that extra cost will translate to willingness to sell. But not all of MC curve is the supply curve. Only the upward sloping part ,which is above the shutdown point , below shutdown point, supply is zero as firm won't supply anything. The reason for upward sloping part comes from the second order condition of profit maximization ($$\frac{\partial^{2} \Pi}{\partial q^{2} } = -TC''=-MC' < 0 $$  ) for concavity of profit which means MC curve should be increasing for profit maximization.   


### Producer's Surplus and Profits in Perfect competition

The definition of producer surplus says it is the net benefit a producer gets from producing the output. As usual the change in surplus always turns out to be a more important thing to us than absolute thing. 
Suppose the firm chooses to produce q*. Now the change in producer surplus will be the change in gains , that is change in profits, as the producer would have also chosen to put out zero output i.e 

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta PS = \Delta\Pi = \Pi(q*) -\Pi(0) = TR-VC 
\end{aligned}
$$


but at zero output the producer surplus would have been 0, so 

$$
\begin{aligned}
&PS  = TC-VC \quad\text{or} \quad PS = \Pi+FC
\end{aligned}
$$

of course, the delta relation $$\Delta PS = \Delta\Pi$$ still holds.

##### Relation between area under Marginal Costs and Variable costs :

$$
MC = \frac{d(TC)}{dq} = \frac{d(VC)}{dq}
\implies \int_0^Q MC(q) = \int_0^Q\frac{d(VC(q))}{dq} \, dq = VC(Q) - VC(0) = VC(Q)
$$

here we used the fundamental theorem of calculus and the fact that VC(0) = 0 , 
(note that here putting here TC instead of VC would have put us into the same conclusion as TC(0) = FC and TC(Q)-FC= VC(Q) )

so the final conclusion is : 

$$ 
\int_0^Q MC(q) \, dq = VC(Q)
$$

so we have three ways to represent the Producer surplus in Perfectly Competitive firm: 
1. REVENUE - VARIABLE COSTS
2. AREA ABOVE THE MC CURVE AND BELOW DEMAND CURVE (as Area below the MC cost curve is VC)
3. AREA TO THE LEFT OF SUPPLY CURVE
